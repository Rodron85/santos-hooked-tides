Tides · Southampton

Line up the tides

Southampton has a double high water: the tide rises to a 1st high, stays high for 2–3 hours, then makes a 2nd high before a quick ebb. Low water is the sharpest point, so the app times everything from it. Low waters are worked out from the moon and tuned to Southampton tide tables (usually within 10 min); a live ocean model is only used if it agrees to within 20 min, because in the Solent it runs about an hour early. The two highs are less exact (within about 30 min). Check Admiralty EasyTide (easytide.admiralty.co.uk) once, type today's first low water here (Southampton) or first high water (elsewhere), and the app lines itself up.

Fishing the tide

  • Springs (a day or two after new and full moon): big range, strong flow, food stirred up. Best for bass, rays, cod and smoothhound.
  • Neaps: gentle flow, clearer water. Easier for float fishing, mullet, wrasse and light lures.
  • Flood tide into high water is the classic shore window in the Solent; the first of the ebb often fishes well too.
  • Southampton's high-water stand between the 1st and 2nd high gives you 2–3 extra hours at the top of the tide. The ebb that follows is fast.

How the moon affects fishing

  • Major periods: about 2 hours around the moon passing overhead or underfoot. Fish feed hardest then.
  • Minor periods: about 1 hour around moonrise and moonset.
  • New and full moon give the strongest days, and they also bring spring tides.
  • When a major or minor period lands on dawn or dusk, it's prime time.
  • For squid at night, a dark sky (new moon, or moon below the horizon) makes pier lights work best.

Rules · what you can keep

Recreational sea angling, Southern IFCA area. Checked 7 Nov 2026.

Bass

42 cmminimum size
3 a dayper angler, Apr–Jan
Feb–Marcatch and release only
  • Applies from the shore and from a boat. No bass may be taken with fixed or drift nets, and bass caught for fun can never be sold.
  • Southampton Water bass nursery area, 30 April to 1 November: from a boat you must not target bass or use sandeel as bait for any fish. Fawley power station outfall: all year. Shore fishing is not covered, but return small bass there.

Pollack

30 cmminimum size
3 a daynew limit for 2026

The UK and EU agreed a recreational limit of 3 pollack per angler per day for 2026. Check gov.uk that it's in force before you keep a fourth.

Must go back

  • European eel: anglers can't keep them (Environment Agency byelaw).
  • Tope: tope caught on rod and line must be released (national rules).
  • Shad (allis and twaite): protected.
  • Undulate ray: endangered; release it alive (Angling Trust advice).
  • Any fish under its minimum size: return it to the sea straight away.

Rigs & knots

The rigs that cover most Solent shore fishing

Two-hook flapper

Whiting, pouting, dab, flounder, dogfish. Size 1–2 hooks, 50–60 lb rig body. Plain lead lets the bait roll for flounder; a grip lead holds in fast tide.

Pulley pennel

Bass, rays, smoothhound and conger over rough ground. The pulley pulls the lead up off the snags when you wind in. Hooks 2/0–4/0.

Running ledger

The fish can take line without feeling the lead. Great for flounder in the estuaries, bass and mullet. Hook size 1 to 2/0 to suit the bait.

Float rig (squid, garfish, mackerel)

Set the depth with the stop knot: 1–3 m for squid under lights, shallower for garfish. Use a size 4–6 hook with a mackerel strip for garfish and mackerel.

Knots

Improved clinch: hooks and swivels on mono

  1. Pass the line through the eye.
  2. Wrap the tag end around the main line 5–7 times.
  3. Push the tag through the small loop by the eye, then back through the big loop.
  4. Wet it, pull tight slowly, trim.

Palomar: best for braid

  1. Double 15 cm of line and push the loop through the eye.
  2. Tie a loose overhand knot with the doubled line.
  3. Pass the hook or swivel through the loop.
  4. Wet it, pull both ends tight, trim.

Uni knot: all-rounder

  1. Pass the line through the eye and lay the tag back alongside, making a loop.
  2. Wrap the tag around both lines inside the loop 5–6 times.
  3. Wet it and pull the tag to snug the turns.
  4. Pull the main line to slide the knot down to the eye. Trim.

Uni-to-uni: main line to shock leader

  1. Overlap the two ends by about 20 cm.
  2. Tie a uni knot with the leader around the main line (5 turns).
  3. Tie a uni knot with the main line around the leader (8–10 turns if it's braid).
  4. Wet both, pull them together, trim close.

Stop knot: for float rigs

  1. Lay a short piece of line or power gum along the main line, making a loop.
  2. Wrap the tag through the loop 4–5 times.
  3. Pull tight and leave the ends 1 cm long so they pass through the rod rings.

Quick guide

  • Shock leader: 10 lb breaking strain for every 1 oz of lead (5 oz lead = 50 lb leader). Always use one when casting hard; a snapped lead can kill.
  • Hooks: size 1–2 for whiting, pouting, dab and flounder; 2–6 for plaice and sole; 6–10 for mullet and bream; 1/0–3/0 for bass and smoothhound; 3/0–6/0 for rays and conger.
  • Leads: grip leads in fast tide (Calshot, Hurst); plain leads to roll a bait in the estuaries.
